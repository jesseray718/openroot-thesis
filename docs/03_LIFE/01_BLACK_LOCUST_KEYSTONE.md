# Black Locust as Keystone Species — Section 5.1

> ⚠️ **NOTE:** Black locust is aggressive and can outcompete native species. Site-specific ecological assessment is required before large-scale planting.

*From THESIS.md Section 5.1*

---

## Why Black Locust (*Robinia pseudoacacia*)

### Nitrogen Fixation
- Fixes 40–100 kg N/ha/year via *Rhizobium* symbiosis
- Reduces or eliminates synthetic nitrogen fertilizer requirements

### Structural Timber
| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Density | 770 kg/m³ |
| Modulus of elasticity | 14–16 GPa |
| Natural durability (EN 350) | Class 1 — 80–100 years in ground contact without treatment |
| Compressive strength | 60–70 MPa |

Suitable for framing, posts, foundation piles, and fuel.

### Fodder and Apiculture
- Flowers: monofloral honey nectar; edible for humans
- Leaves: 21–24% crude protein (high-quality fodder)

### Rapid Growth
- Reaches harvestable diameter (12–15cm) in 8–12 years
- Coppices vigorously: 20–25 year coppice rotation cycles

---

## Proposed Planting

**Density:** 50–200 trees/acre in alley cropping configuration  
**Annual yield (established):** 5–10 cords wood + nitrogen fertility + fodder

---

## Alternatives for Sensitive Ecosystems

- Honey locust (*Gleditsia triacanthos*)
- Alder (*Alnus* spp.)
- Local *Acacia* species
- Siberian pea shrub (*Caragana arborescens*) — cold climates
